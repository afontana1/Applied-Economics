# Source index and reading pathways

This index describes what is actually in the uploaded books and where each book fits into the **proposed** curriculum. Chapter references are to **printed chapter labels** and may differ from PDF page indices. The archived S&B book is scanned/image-based; its outline was checked visually against the printed contents pages and the bundled answers pamphlet.


> **How to use this file:** The [README](README.md) contains the complete 24-week schedule and its labs/capstones; the [mathematical reference](mathematical-reference.md) summarizes formulas and proof conditions. This index is only for locating material in the supplied books. PDF links expect the original textbook files to remain next to these three Markdown guides in the `core-math/` directory; the three-file download does not redistribute the books.

## Simon and Blume — *Mathematics for Economists*

**Primary sequence.** [Open book](Carl%20P.%20Simon%2C%20Lawrence%20E.%20Blume%20-%20Mathematics%20for%20economists.pdf) · [Open answer pamphlet](Mathematics-for-Economists-SOLUTION.pdf)

| Chapters | Contents as indexed in the supplied edition | Program role |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | Introduction; one-variable calculus foundations, applications, chain rule, exponents/logarithms | Weeks 1–2 |
| 6–11 | Introduction to linear algebra; linear systems; matrices; determinants; Euclidean spaces; linear independence | Weeks 3–4 |
| 12–15 | Limits/open sets; functions of several variables; multivariable calculus; implicit functions | Weeks 5–6, 18 |
| 16–19 | Quadratic forms; unconstrained optimization; constrained optimization I and II | Weeks 7–9 |
| 20–22 | Homogeneous/homothetic; concave/quasiconcave; economic applications | Weeks 10–12 |
| 23 | Eigenvalues and eigenvectors | Weeks 4, 14, 17 |
| 24–25 | Ordinary differential equations: scalar and systems | Weeks 13–14, 21 |
| 26–28 | Detailed determinants; matrix-associated subspaces; applications of linear independence | Weeks 3–4 advanced review |
| 29–30 | Limits/compact sets; calculus of several variables II | Weeks 10, 18 |
| Appendices 1–5 | Sets, proofs, trigonometry, complex numbers, integral calculus, introduction to probability | Prerequisite repair and Weeks 15, 19 |

The pamphlet includes solutions/answers for many assigned exercises, not necessarily every exercise or a complete proof of every theorem. The attached file has text-extraction artifacts; for ambiguous formula glyphs, consult the original PDF page rather than the extracted text.

**Scope caution:** Garrity surveys advanced mathematics and often introduces motivating constructions. Completing a single chapter does not imply full mastery of an entire graduate subject (e.g., measure theory or differential geometry).

## Scott E. Page — *The Model Thinker*

**Model-building and interdisciplinary applications.** [Open book](The%20Model%20Thinker%20What%20You%20Need%20to%20Know%20to%20Make%20Data%20Work%20for%20You.pdf)

| Chapters | Topic | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Many-model thinking; why model; science of models; human actors | Week 23 and overall modeling lens |
| 5–6 | Normal and power-law distributions | Weeks 15–16 |
| 7–9 | Linear models; concavity/convexity; value and power | Weeks 7, 10–11 |
| 10–11 | Networks; broadcast/diffusion/contagion | Week 20 |
| 12–14 | Entropy, random walks, path dependence | Weeks 16–17 |
| 15–19 | Local interactions, Lyapunov equilibria, Markov models, systems dynamics, threshold feedback | Weeks 14, 17, 20–21 |
| 20 | Spatial and hedonic choice | Week 11 optional |
| 21–24 | Game theory, cooperation, collective action, mechanism design | Week 22 |
| 25–27 | Signaling, learning, multi-armed bandits | Week 22 optional modeling lab |
| 28–29 | Rugged landscapes; inequality and model humility | Weeks 21, 23–24 |

**Scope caution:** This is a conceptual modeling book rather than a substitute for formal mathematical analysis or a full econometrics textbook. Its examples are used to compare model mechanisms and communicate limitations.

## Priority reading pathway

- **Mathematical economics:** S&B 2–23, then 24–25, with G 1–4 as reinforcement.
- **Econometrics:** S&B 6–16, 23, 29–30; G 2–4 and 15; P 5–7, 12–14.
- **Financial economics:** S&B 6–23; G 1–3 and 15; P 5–6, 13, 17–19.
- **Modeling complexity:** P 1–29, with S&B 23–25, G 15–16, and any required graph/dynamics review.
- **Pure-math enrichment:** G 5–14 in a separate proof-intensive sequence with additional specialized textbooks if necessary.

The guide intentionally does **not** give chapter-to-chapter equivalences where the texts cover related concepts at very different levels of rigor.
